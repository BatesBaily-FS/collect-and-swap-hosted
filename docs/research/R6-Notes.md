## Sub-Topic 1 - Establishing Project Validation Practices

A robust business case is more than just an outline of what will be built; it demonstrates why it should be built and how it is valuable to the project as a whole. Creating a business case is a systematic processed designed to justify a proposed project or investment and a completed one should include:

- A clear statement of the problem/opportunity and what will happen if its not addressed. It is important when making this statement that it is clearly communicated, accurate, and is realistic to your project.

- Well-documented assumptions and analysis of possible solutions and risks. Create a thorough list of assumptions related to market conditions, customer behavior, cost estimates, and revenue projections and compare them to historical data and industry benchmarks to ensure they are realistic. It is best to provide a best-case, worst-case, and most likely scenarios.

- A validation plan with tactics for confirming that feature meets both user expectations and business goals. This entails a thorough evaluation of all options for improvement, verifying estimates and cost projections, and weighing the goal vs time, money, and resources used. Additionally performing a risk assessment and assessing market and competitive analysis.

## Sub-Topic 2 - Tools, Technologies, and Methods of Validation

For future implementation into my app, I researched and am preparing to integrate the following Google tools and practices, all of which are widely used for strengthening business cases in digital products:

- Google Analytics/Analytics 360: Set up for tracking future engagement in my case - how often users propose trades, click "Book Clubs", or use the event locator. This gives evidence of real-world use and identifies weak points or unused features.

- A/B Testing(via Google Analytics): Plan to compare variations of interface designs such as different placements of the Book Club Tab or varying styles and locations of search bars - to see which promotes higher engagement, providing real world data to base decisions on.

- Use Case Testing: Drafting use cases from user's perspectives examples include "User proposes a trade" or "User joins a club" and laying out the work flow of the task and verifying it matches the desired approach.

## Sub-Topic 3 - Planning Validation into the Workflow

Validation is an integrated, ongoing activity that ensures every feature - especially ones critical to the users experience are actively assessed and refined throughout the projects lifecycle. This week, my planning is focused on taking the first step into integrating tools such as Google Analytics and A/B testing.

- I am using Github Issues and Project Boards to document and track each validation initiative, such as analytics and A/B testing for future milestones. This creates clear accountability and makes it easy to monitor progress, review priorities, and share updated.

- For each type of validation - like adding Google Analytics or preparing A/B tests - I create specific issues that outline what will be implemented, why it matters, and the expected impact. This helps ensure validation tasks are not overlooked and are tied directly to improving user experience and business objectives.

- Results from analytics review and A/B testing will be summarized and linked to relevant issues, allowing insight to inform future development and feature improvements. By including validation finding into the workflow and documentation, the project remains responsive to real-world user data rather than speculative planning.
